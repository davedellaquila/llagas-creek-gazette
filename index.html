A House Gets Ready: Rodents First, Then Everything Else

From Kay Korbel’s reports to the sellers, late September – November 3, 2026

The Llagas Creek house is being readied for sale, and the work has a strict order to it. Kay’s sequence: the house must be cleared of rodents first — fumigation can’t happen until it is — then fumigation, the Section One pest repairs, the plumbing repairs, exterior paint, interior paint, cleaning, staging, and at last photography. No asking price or list date has been set; this paper exists to keep the record straight while the house gets there.

The biggest job so far is done and paid: Silicon Valley Rodent Proofers’ $5,800 proofing of the roof vents, eaves, attic and crawl space was invoiced October 18 and marked paid. But “paid” isn’t “finished” — the trappers keep checking until the house is clear, and Kay was still chasing that confirmation on October 29. Everything downstream waits on it: the signed $1,020 of extra crawl-space work at Clean Crawl Space is deliberately not scheduled yet, on Kay’s instruction, until the rodent crew clears the sub area and seals the entry points. Scheduling is expected in early December.

Money is moving in smaller pieces, too. Clean Crawl Space’s two October invoices — $1,125 for the sump pumps and $1,800 for pulling down the vapor barrier — were paid by Ron. Kay fronted $3,064.18 of early costs out of her own pocket. And her October expense plan projects $83,324.18 of third-party work all-in before the house is sold — paint, pest repairs, cleaning, staging and photography included.

Two bills need the brothers’ attention now. Nelson’s Pool shows $3,881 due for the new spa heater and monthly service — but Kay’s own instruction, from October 22, is to pay the heater only after Mike Nelson tests the system once the security spa cover is installed, expected mid-November. And the city water bill — $652.13, due November 10 — is not on autopay, whatever anyone remembers. It has to be paid by hand. Details in the boxes below.
